# 6. How the algorithm thinks (and a growth plan for under 100 subscribers)

*Output of prompt #6.*

## The one-sentence version

YouTube tries to show each viewer the video they're most likely to click **and be
glad they watched**. Every metric below is a way of measuring one half of that
sentence.

## The metrics in plain English

| Metric | What it means | What it tells you | Rough "healthy" range for a small B2B channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Impressions** | How many times your thumbnail was shown | How much YouTube is willing to test you | Grows as CTR + watch time prove themselves |
| **Click-through rate (CTR)** | % of impressions that became a click | Is the title + thumbnail compelling *to the people it was shown to*? | 4–8% (search traffic often higher; browse lower) |
| **Average view duration (AVD)** | Average minutes watched per view | Did the video hold attention? | 3–5 min on a 10-min video |
| **Audience retention** | The graph of who's still watching, second by second | *Where* people leave. The most useful diagnostic you have. | 60%+ still watching at 30 s; flat-ish line after |
| **Returning viewers** | People who've watched you before | Are you building an audience or getting one-off visits? | Grows steadily; spikes after series videos |
| **Search traffic** | Views from YouTube search | Evergreen demand; your best source early on | Should be your #1 source for months 1–3 |
| **Browse features** | Home page & subscriptions feed | YouTube recommending you to people not searching | Grows once CTR + retention are proven |
| **Suggested videos** | Shown next to/after other videos | YouTube thinks you're a good "next video" | Grows with series/playlists and topic clusters |

**Important:** CTR and retention pull against each other. A clickbait title raises CTR
but tanks retention when the video doesn't deliver, and YouTube notices. Honest
titles that over-deliver win long-term.

### Search vs browse: why it matters for us

- **Search** = someone typed a problem ("google business profile not showing").
  These viewers are high-intent and **far more likely to become leads**. This is
  our priority traffic.
- **Browse/suggested** = YouTube pushed you. Bigger volume, lower intent. Good for
  growth, less so for leads.

So early on, **title for search** ("How to…", "Why your… isn't…") and let browse
come later.

## Growth strategy for a channel with under 100 subscribers

### How often to post
**1 long video + 3 Shorts per week, on a fixed day.** Consistency beats volume.
Missing a week does nothing terrible to "the algorithm", but it breaks the habit.
Never drop quality to hit a schedule. Skip a week instead.

### What to test first (in this order)
1. **Titles & thumbnails** (affects CTR). Use YouTube's built-in *Test & Compare* to
   A/B test up to 3 thumbnails (and titles where available).
2. **The first 30 seconds** (affects retention). Try: result-first vs problem-first openings.
3. **Topics/pillars**: which of the 5 pillars brings views *and* enquiries.
4. **Length**: 8 min vs 12 min on similar topics.
5. **CTA placement**: mid-video soft CTA vs end-only.

Change **one thing at a time** and give it 7–14 days (small channels need time for
data to mean anything).

### How to read results
- **Low impressions** → topic has no demand, or titles don't match searches. Check
  YouTube autocomplete for the phrase.
- **Impressions fine, CTR low (<3%)** → title/thumbnail problem. Rework it.
- **CTR fine, retention drops sharply in the first 30 s** → the opening doesn't
  match the promise. Re-cut or re-film the intro next time.
- **Retention fine, few subscribers** → no reason to come back. Mention the series,
  what's next week.
- **Views fine, no enquiries** → CTA, lead magnet or landing page problem (see
  [09-monetisation-and-leads.md](09-monetisation-and-leads.md)).

> At under 1,000 views per video, single numbers are noisy. Look at the trend
> across 3–4 videos, not one.

### Turning viewers into subscribers
- Say *why* to subscribe, specifically: "Every Tuesday I fix one real local business's
  website or Google profile. Subscribe if you want to see whose is next."
- Ask after you've delivered value (around 60–70%), not at the start.
- Build series. People subscribe to "what's next", not to a single video.
- Reply to every comment in the first 48 hours, which also tells YouTube the video sparks conversation.

### Turning viewers into enquiries
- One **specific** free offer per video, matched to the topic (website video →
  website check; AI policy video → policy template).
- Say it out loud **and** pin it as the first comment **and** put it in line 1 of the description.
- Make the next step tiny: "Send us your website on WhatsApp and we'll send back
  three fixes." Lower friction beats bigger offers.
- Show real work. "Fix It Live" videos are the single best lead format, because
  viewers think "do mine".

## 5 common mistakes new business channels make

1. **Making ads, not videos.** "Here's what our agency does" gets no views. Teach
   the thing; the selling happens on its own.
2. **Targeting everyone.** "AI explained" competes with millions. "AI for dental
   practices in the UK" competes with almost no one, and that viewer can buy.
3. **Long intros.** Logo animation, "hi guys, welcome back", agenda slide. Half the
   audience is gone by second 20.
4. **Judging by subscribers.** A 400-subscriber channel that brings 10 enquiries a
   month beats a 40k channel that brings none. Track leads from day one.
5. **Quitting at video 6.** Most channels quit before they've published enough for
   YouTube (or themselves) to learn anything. Commit to 13 videos before judging.
