## 1:30 Step 1: Pick ONE annoying, repetitive job (1:30–3:15)

> Grab a pen. Write down the three jobs in your business that are: one, done every
> week; two, mostly the same each time; and three, nobody enjoys.
>
> Things like answering the same customer questions, writing quotes, chasing
> invoices, typing up meeting notes, writing job adverts.

**[SCREEN]** simple 3-column table: Job / Hours per week / Same every time?

> Now pick the one with the most hours. That's your first AI project. Not five.
> One.
>
> Here's a real example. A practice we worked with was spending around six hours a
> week replying to the same booking questions by email. That became the first
> project. Nothing fancy.

**[TEXT]** "Most hours + most repetitive = start here"
**[SFX]** pen tick

## 5:10 Step 3: Write three simple rules (5:10–6:45)

> Before anyone starts, write three rules. One page, max.
>
> One: what data must **never** go into AI: customer health records, card details,
> anything you'd be embarrassed to see leaked.
> Two: a human checks everything before it goes to a customer.
> Three: which tools are approved, and nothing else.

## 6:45 Step 4: Measure it for 30 days (6:45–8:45)

> Here's the step everyone skips. Measure it.
>
> Before you start, write down how long the job takes now. Six hours a week? Write
> it down. Then run the AI version for thirty days and write down how long it takes
> now, and whether the quality held up.

**[SCREEN]** simple before/after table: "Before: 6 hrs/wk → After: ? hrs/wk"

> If it saved time and quality held, roll it out and pick the next job. If it didn't,
> stop. That's not failure, that's the system working. You've lost a month and
> nothing else.
>
> The businesses that get real value from AI aren't the ones with the fanciest tools.
> They're the ones that do this boring loop: pick a job, try it safely, measure, repeat.

**[TEXT]** "Pick → Try → Measure → Repeat"

## Drop-off risks and how the script fixes them

| Moment | Why viewers might leave | Fix built into the script |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 s | Looks like another generic AI video | Opens with a contrarian claim ("most start wrong"), no logo intro |
| 0:15–0:40 | "Who is this guy?" credentials ramble | Kept to 25 s; credibility through honesty ("a third of the time, not yet") |
| ~2:00 | Step 1 feels like homework | Concrete example (6 hrs/week of booking emails) + the "new starter" test |
| ~3:30 | Talking-head fatigue | Switches to screen recording of real Copilot/Gemini |
| ~5:00 | Midpoint slump | Explicit teaser for step 4 |
| ~6:15 | Rules feel dry | Visual "bad example" + light SFX |
| ~8:30 | Viewer thinks "got it" and leaves | Promised "one question" is still unpaid; payoff held until 8:45 |
| End | Outro = exit | No outro; go straight to the next video recommendation |
